# Expiration estimation

Implements spec.md: Expiration and Food Safety.
Prefer dates explicitly supplied by the user or readable on a label. A vague user date is source user with precision approximate. When absent, estimate a calendar expiration date, source ai and precision approximate. Distinguish safety use-by from quality best-by.

For uncertain dates, actively maximize the estimated usable lifetime within a credible range. Assume the item was recently purchased, in normal good condition, promptly stored, and kept under the proper conditions unless the user or image indicates otherwise. Select the longest credible endpoint, not the minimum, midpoint, typical average, or conservative recommendation. For example, if credible guidance spans 3 to 14 days for the plausible item states, use 14 days when nothing establishes a short-lived state. Do not subtract days merely because details are missing and do not add a precautionary buffer. For produce and other foods whose decline is primarily freshness or quality, set a generous quality-review date rather than an early discard date. The user accepts texture and flavor decline and prefers fewer premature expiration warnings.

This long-end policy applies to all general estimates, including produce, dairy, packaged foods, condiments, ingredients, and leftovers where a range is defensible. The only exception is a safety-sensitive refrigerated food with an established latest safe-storage limit; do not knowingly extend such a safety date or turn uncertainty into a claim of safety. Leftovers in the Fridge need the latest defensible end of the applicable safety range based on food and known storage, not the earliest end. Do not infer safety from smell or appearance.

Do not treat the generic word "salad" as a prepared mayonnaise-, egg-, meat-, seafood-, pasta-, or deli salad. Determine which case applies from the message or image: unopened packaged greens or salad kit, loose whole greens, an undressed cut-vegetable salad, or a prepared/dressed/protein salad. For unopened packaged greens or kits, prefer a readable package date; without one, use the optimistic upper end appropriate to that specific product and packaging. Whole or loose produce uses its own produce lifetime. Undressed cut vegetables use their applicable quality window. Only apply the short prepared-salad safety window when dressing, cooked ingredients, eggs, meat, seafood, dairy, or leftover preparation is stated or visible. If the case materially changes the date and cannot be inferred, ask one concise clarification and return no inventory action rather than defaulting to the shortest case.

Plain leafy greens, lettuce, spinach, cabbage, herbs, and similar raw produce are not prepared salads or leftovers. Never assign them a 3- or 4-day prepared-salad date solely because they are described as "salad," "green leaf," "greens," or an imprecise produce name. With no evidence that they are dressed, cooked, cut long ago, spoiled, or mixed with safety-sensitive ingredients, use the generous upper end for fresh properly refrigerated produce and classify the date as quality, not safety.

For items assigned to Freezer, assume continuous storage at 0°F (-18°C) or below unless contrary information is supplied. AI-estimated freezer dates are quality review dates, never safety deadlines. Use the generous upper end of item-specific freezer quality guidance. When no product-specific quality duration can be inferred, default to a quality review date 12 months from addition and disclose that assumption. Do not mark a continuously frozen item unsafe merely because its quality date passed; after thawing, apply the appropriate refrigerated handling limit.

Clearly expired safety-dated items must be marked expired. Do not silently turn an expired item available; only an explicit user correction to expiration permits this. Never recommend safety-expired items for consumption.
